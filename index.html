Featured SDG Project

Community Rainwater Harvesting & Urban Farming Initiative

SDG 2SDG 6SDG 11SDG 13
Status: Concept stage — seeking pilot partner

Problem Statement

Food & beverages made up 15.7% of average Malaysian household spending in 2024, meaning grocery costs are a significant, recurring pressure on many households. At the same time, most Malaysian homes rely entirely on treated piped water even for non-potable uses like gardening, while Malaysia's heavy monsoon rainfall goes largely uncaptured. Combining the two — capturing free rainwater to grow food locally — offers a way to ease both pressures at once. This challenge mirrors what I have observed in parts of Tamil Nadu, India, where seasonal water stress and rising food costs affect similar low-income communities, reinforcing the case for a solution that could eventually be adapted across both regions.

The Solution

A community-driven system that captures rainwater for reuse in shared urban vegetable and herb gardens, paired with environmental education — and a digital dashboard that turns this from a standalone gardening project into an IT-driven impact-tracking system.

Physical system:

  • Low-cost rainwater harvesting systems (gutters, tanks, basic filtration).
  • Shared vegetable & herb gardens irrigated with harvested water.
  • Environmental workshops for students and residents.

System flow:

Rainwater → Storage → Irrigation → Community Garden → Digital Dashboard

The digital layer (where my IT background comes in):

A dashboard designed to track project data such as:

  • Litres of rainwater captured
  • Litres of treated (piped) water displaced
  • Kg of produce harvested
  • Number of participating households
  • Workshops conducted
  • Estimated system cost and measured savings

This is the part of the project I plan to actually build and demo — a lightweight web app that community coordinators could use to log tank levels and harvests, turning a sustainability initiative into a data-backed, IT-supported one.

Target Location

Pilot site: a PPR (Projek Perumahan Rakyat) flat community in Kuala Lumpur. PPR flats are purpose-built low-cost housing, making them a relevant setting to test whether a low-cost community-scale intervention can deliver measurable benefits.

Personal Statement — SDG Innovation Summit Malaysia 2026

I am a third-year Information Technology undergraduate from Tamil Nadu, India, drawn to the SDGs through a simple observation: many of the water and food security challenges I've seen growing up mirror what communities across South and Southeast Asia face. My primary project is a Community Rainwater Harvesting & Urban Farming Initiative, designed for Malaysia's PPR flat communities.

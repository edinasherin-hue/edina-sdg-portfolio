Featured SDG Project

Community Rainwater Harvesting & Urban Farming Initiative

SDG 2SDG 6SDG 11SDG 13
Status: Concept stage — seeking pilot partner

Problem Statement

Food & beverages made up 15.7% of average Malaysian household spending in 2024, and B40 households (earning RM5,906/month or below) feel this most. At the same time, most Malaysian homes rely entirely on treated piped water even for non-potable uses like gardening, while heavy monsoon rainfall goes largely uncaptured — despite rainwater harvesting being able to cut a household's water bill by 30–50%. Combining the two — capturing free rainwater to grow food locally — addresses both cost pressures at once. This challenge mirrors what I have observed in parts of Tamil Nadu, India, where seasonal water stress and rising food costs affect similar low-income communities, reinforcing the case for a solution that could eventually be adapted across both regions.

The Solution

A community-driven system that captures rainwater for reuse in shared urban vegetable and herb gardens, paired with environmental education.

  • Low-cost rainwater harvesting systems (gutters, tanks, basic filtration).
  • Shared vegetable & herb gardens irrigated with harvested water.
  • Environmental workshops for students and residents.
  • Impact tracking: water saved, food produced, and cost savings.

Target Location

Pilot site: a PPR (Projek Perumahan Rakyat) flat community in Kuala Lumpur — purpose-built low-cost housing for B40 households, making it a strong fit for a low-cost, high-impact pilot.

Other Projects

E-Waste Drop-off Locator & Awareness Web App

SDG 11SDG 12
Status: Concept / early prototype

Malaysia's household e-waste recycling rate stays below 25%, and the country is on track to generate 24.5 million units of e-waste in a single year — with only around 1 in 5 units properly recovered. Most residents simply don't know where their nearest legal drop-off point is.

A simple web app (map + search) showing nearby DOE-registered e-waste collection points and retailer take-back schemes, with short guides on what counts as e-waste and why it matters.

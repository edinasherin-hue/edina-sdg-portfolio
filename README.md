## Featured Project: Community Rainwater Harvesting & Urban Farming Initiative

### SDGs Addressed
- **SDG 2** – Zero Hunger
- **SDG 6** – Clean Water and Sanitation
- **SDG 11** – Sustainable Cities and Communities
- **SDG 13** – Climate Action

### Problem Statement
Food & beverages made up 15.7% of average Malaysian household spending in 2024, and B40 households (earning RM5,906/month or below) feel this most, since a larger share of their income goes to basic needs. At the same time, most Malaysian homes rely entirely on treated piped water even for non-potable uses like gardening, while heavy monsoon rainfall goes largely uncaptured — despite rainwater harvesting being able to cut a household's water bill by 30–50%. Combining the two — capturing free rainwater to grow food locally — addresses both cost pressures at once. This challenge mirrors what I have observed in parts of Tamil Nadu, India, where seasonal water stress and rising food costs affect similar low-income communities, reinforcing the case for a solution that could eventually be adapted across both regions.

### The Solution
A community-driven system that captures rainwater for reuse in shared urban vegetable and herb gardens, paired with environmental education for residents and students.

- Low-cost rainwater harvesting systems (gutters, storage tanks, basic filtration)
- Shared vegetable & herb gardens irrigated with harvested water
- Environmental workshops for students and residents
- Impact tracking dashboard: water saved, food produced, cost savings

### Target Location
Pilot site: a PPR (Projek Perumahan Rakyat) flat community in Kuala Lumpur. PPR flats are purpose-built low-cost housing for B40 households, making them a strong fit for a low-cost, high-impact pilot.

| Vegetables & herbs produced | ≈200 kg/year | 100 m² growing area × 2 kg/m²/year planning assumption |
| Equivalent retail value of produce | ≈RM1,000–RM2,000/year | 200 kg × assumed RM5–RM10/kg equivalent retail value |
| Households/community members reached | To be measured during pilot | Actual number of participating households |
| Water actually saved | To be measured during pilot | Tank meter/usage records compared with irrigation demand |

## Other Projects

### E-Waste Drop-off Locator & Awareness Web App
- **SDGs:** SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities), SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption & Production)
- Malaysia's household e-waste recycling rate stays below 25%, and the country is on track to generate 24.5 million units of e-waste in a single year — with only around 1 in 5 units properly recovered. Most residents simply don't know where their nearest legal drop-off point is.
- A simple web app (map + search) that shows nearby DOE-registered e-waste collection points and retailer take-back schemes, plus short guides on what counts as e-waste and why it matters.
- **Status:** Concept stage / early prototype — a good fit for a student-built MVP using a public map API and a manually compiled list of collection points to start.
